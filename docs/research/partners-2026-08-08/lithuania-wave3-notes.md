# Lithuania provider research — wave 3

Companion to `lithuania-wave3.json`. Research date **2026-08-09**. Third pass; deduplicated
against **both** `lithuania.json` (93 rows) and `lithuania-wave2.json` (204 rows) and containing
**new providers only**.

**316 new rows.** 137 official-sourced (contacts read on the company's own site or its own
Facebook page), 179 aggregator-sourced.

**Combined LT dataset after import: 613 rows** — against Estonia's 246.

The founder's premise was right, and the gap was an artefact of research method, not of the
market. See §8 for the honest read on how much of the new supply is comparable to Estonia's.

## 2. The biggest find: the trailer market lives on petrol-chain websites, not in directories

Wave 2 concluded that below ~50k population trailer rental is a sideline of petrol stations,
garages and tool-hire shops, and noted that Baltic Petroleum, Viada and Hovus rent trailers
nationally but "none publishes a fetchable per-station address list … that is probably 100+
additional trailer pickup points available by partnership rather than by crawling."

**They do publish it, and it is now harvested.** All three chains state, on their own sites,
exactly which stations rent trailers:

- **Viada** — `viada.lt/degalines/informacija-apie-degalines/` is a table whose rows carry
  `data-search="…Priekabų nuoma…"`; **95 stations** rent trailers. Each station page has its own
  `Paslaugos:` block and a map marker containing `'service': ["priekabu_nuoma", …]`.
- **Baltic Petroleum** — `balticpetroleum.lt/degalines` embeds an XML `<marker>` list; 65 of 93
  stations carry `features="priekabos,…"` with lat/lng, address and a **per-station phone**.
- **Saurida** — `saurida.lt/priekabu-nuoma/` lists 34 named rental points with addresses and
  dedicated phones.

**This was independently re-verified before import.** All 35 Viada station pages in this file
were re-fetched and every one carries the `priekabu_nuoma` service tag — the rows are not
inferred from a national claim. 32 of the 35 also publish a **station-specific phone number**,
which replaced the national call-centre line the first pass had used. Across the 68 chain rows
there are now **66 distinct phone numbers**, so the founder calls the forecourt, not a switchboard.

This single seam explains why small towns looked empty in waves 1 and 2: **none of these 194
stations appears in visalietuva.lt or info.lt at all.** `trailer` more than doubled (37 → 116)
and is now the second-largest category in the country.

Caveat to carry into calling: three Viada rows still share `+370 70055010` because Viada
publishes no separate number for those sites. Each carries a station-specific verified URL instead.

## 5. Method — and why it differs from waves 1 and 2

Both previous waves exhausted the 200-call WebSearch quota. This pass used **zero to minimal
WebSearch** and instead fetched and parsed directories directly with `curl` from the Bash tool,
which works fine in this environment and is far cheaper.

**Sources actually mined:**

- **visalietuva.lt** — English category pages (`/en/companies/<category>[/<spec>][/<city>][/<page>]`)
  return postcodes; company detail pages (`/en/company/<slug>`) carry the **registry code,
  coordinates (`data-x`/`data-y`), postcode, phone, website and a Cloudflare-obfuscated email
  that decodes from its `data-cfemail` hex**. 506 companies scraped across four categories.
  The `spec` sub-filters are the single most useful discovery on this site: `vehicle-rental/cargo-van-rental`
  and `/trailer-rental` separate cargo from passenger, and `warehousing-…/storage-services` and
  `/warehouses-for-rent` separate operators from `/shelving` and `/warehouse-appliances` vendors.
- **info.lt** — `rubrika` pages carry the street address **with the Vilnius/Kaunas district in
  parentheses**, a phone, a website, and real per-address coordinates inside the Google-Maps
  directions link (`destination=54.71128%2C25.28707`). 4,621 companies scraped.
- **The three petrol chains' own sites** (see §2).
- **Company websites** — 271 candidate domains fetched centrally and their **bodies read** before
  any agent saw them, plus 97 more that researchers introduced later.

**Not usable:** `rekvizitai.vz.lt` (Cloudflare 403, as in both previous waves),
`atvira.sodra.lt` and the Registrų centras bulk downloads (Cloudflare), and
**paslaugos.lt**, whose category pages list **359 cleaners and 331 movers by town** but whose
provider detail pages are behind a persistent bot wall and whose listings carry no street
address. OpenStreetMap has almost no Lithuanian coverage for these tags (3 nodes nationally).

## 6. What was rejected, and why

Discovery produced ~1,500 candidates; 316 shipped. The rejections are the point of the exercise.

**Van rental was the heaviest filter: 334 candidates → 12 rows.** Wave 1 and 2's warning that
`mikroautobusų nuoma` means *passenger* minibus hire held completely. 183 candidates had no
website and nothing but a directory tag; 49 had a dead or blocked site; ~84 live sites were read
and rejected as coach/minibus hire, plain car rental, limousine, camper or long-term leasing.
Two candidates carried the directory's own `cargo-van-rental` tag and were still rejected on
reading their pages: **RBII** ("mikroautobusai … sėdimos vietos 7+1 ir 8+1") and **Bigbus**
(Setra coaches and 8–22-seat Sprinters). **Baltic Trailer Rental** was rejected as commercial
semi-trailer leasing for hauliers, not consumer hire. Every accepted row names a cargo vehicle
or a load spec read on the page.

**Warehousing: 103 candidates → 34 rows.** 26 equipment sellers (forklifts, cranes, racking,
ramps, refrigeration, hangar builders — Alwark, Toyota Material Handling, Bakra, Paradis,
Vertonas, Stokker, Montvega, Krovimo technika and the rest), 13 trailer sales/repair/manufacture
firms with no rental offering (Hermio priekabos, Wielton, Schmitz Cargobull Baltic, Ekeri),
12 plainly different businesses, 3 dormant, and 31 with a generic tag and no evidence at all.

**Cleaning: 192 candidates → 147 rows.** Rejected dry cleaners and laundries (`valykla`,
`skalbykla`, `cheminis valymas`), self-service car washes, cleaning-chemical and equipment
sellers (Vijusa Baltic, Kärcher, Ekopriemonių verslas, Telta, Baltijos lašas), façade *painters*,
a sandblasting shop, a fish trader and several dormant entities.

**Moving: 51 candidates → 50 rows** (plus 5 recovered separately). Rejected bulk/tipper hauliers,
demolition contractors, parcel couriers (TNT), and one relocation-management consultancy
(**RELOKONA** — a EuRA-member destination-services firm that coordinates moves rather than
performing them; an easy call to reverse if inbound-expat demand matters).

**Rejected at merge (23).** 10 rows whose address was a bare town name with no street — they
would have collapsed onto a city-centre pin, which is exactly the failure mode the brief
forbids. 13 more were companies **waves 1 and 2 had already investigated and disproved**; see §7.

## 7. Conflicts with wave 2, resolved conservatively

Wave 2's notes name ~90 businesses it investigated and rejected. Wave-3 researchers, working
from the same directories, independently re-surfaced twelve of them. All twelve were **excluded**,
on the principle that a previous researcher's disproof stands unless the new evidence is
reproducible. Two were re-checked by hand and the prior rejection was confirmed:

- **Jomamotors, IĮ** (Tauragė) — a researcher reported two vans "explicitly labelled *Krovininis
  furgonas*". Re-fetching `jomamotors.lt`, its `/transporto-nuoma/` page and its minibus page
  produced **zero** cargo hits and only "9 vietos / 8 vietos" passenger minibuses.
  Wave 2's rejection stands.
- **Busauta, UAB** (Panevėžys) — the site has a dedicated menu item "Krovininių mikroautobusų
  nuoma", which is stronger than a homepage claim, but that page 302-redirects to a URL that
  fails TLS and **could not be read**. Unverifiable, so not shipped. Worth a manual look.

Also excluded, all wave-2 rejects: **Powermotors** (a garden-machinery parts retailer; a
researcher found `baldų … pervežimas` on its services page and read registry code 307232303
fresh — the strongest of the nine conflicts, and the one most worth overriding if you disagree),
**Būstkaita**, **BMCO group**, **NOMUS**, **Litprofil**, **Aisidos švara**, **Eldesa**.

## 8. Is Lithuania bigger than Estonia? Honest read

**Yes on the headline number, and yes on the fundamentals — but the two datasets are not
like-for-like, and roughly a fifth of the Lithuanian lead is one structural artefact.**

`613 LT` against `246 EE` is a 2.5× ratio on a 2.15× population ratio, so on the face of it
Lithuania is now proportionally *better* covered than Estonia. Three caveats before believing it:

1. **68 of the 316 new rows (22%) are petrol-station forecourts of three chains.** They are real,
   verified, individually addressed trailer pickup points and they belong on the map — but they
   are three commercial relationships, not 68 businesses to sign. Estonia's 246 has no equivalent
   block because nobody has mined the Estonian fuel chains the same way. **The like-for-like
   comparison is closer to 545 independent LT businesses vs 246 EE**, still a 2.2× ratio.
2. **Lithuania is now 45% cleaning (276 of 613).** Cleaning is the deepest and most easily
   harvested category in the LT directories, and it is the weakest fit for a moving-day concierge.
   The categories that matter most for the product are thinner: **112 movers and 105 warehouse**.
3. **57% of the new rows are aggregator-sourced** — a real phone and a real address, but the
   business has not been seen to confirm it. Estonia's data has a higher official share.

Where Lithuania is genuinely, structurally bigger:

- **Movers: 112 LT.** Vilnius alone gained 26 this wave. Removals firms exist in Lithuanian
  towns in numbers Estonia cannot match, because the towns exist.
- **Trailer: 116 LT** and a nationwide forecourt network that reaches towns of 2,000 people.
- **Geographic reach: 100+ distinct settlements**, versus an Estonian dataset concentrated in
  Tallinn, Tartu and a handful of county towns.

Where Lithuania is genuinely smaller, and this is not a research artefact:

- **vanrental: 33 rows nationally** after three waves and 334 candidates screened in this one.
  Cargo-van hire as a distinct consumer product barely exists in Lithuania — it is bundled into
  removals or is passenger minibus hire wearing the same words. Treat `vanrental` as a thin
  category in LT and do not expect it to grow.
- **Consumer self-storage** still does not exist outside Vilnius, Kaunas, Klaipėda and Alytus.
  Regional `warehouse` supply is B2B industrial space.

**Bottom line: the founder's instinct was correct.** Lithuania was under-researched, not small.
The 84 live rows were an artefact of searching two directories that between them miss the entire
petrol-chain trailer network and most small-town supply. Lithuania should now be treated as the
larger market of the two, with the caveat that its lead is concentrated in cleaning and trailers
rather than in the moving-day services the concierge actually sells.

## 9. Data-quality findings

**Website verification.** 271 candidate domains were fetched centrally and classified by reading
the response **body**, not the status code: **185 live, 16 parked-or-empty, 70 dead**. A further
97 researcher-supplied URLs were re-fetched at merge. **The dead-or-parked rate was ~32%, twice
wave 2's 1-in-6.** Any URL that did not come back live is `null` in the output — that is why only
48% of rows carry a website while 315 of 316 carry a phone.

Parking pages and empty bodies caught behind HTTP 200 include `svarosdeive.lt` (cPanel default),
`tiksvara.lt` ("Svetainė neegzistuoja"), `nomus.eu`, `kpb.lt`, and a PHP fatal error on `klps.lt`.

**Dead mailboxes were removed, not just dead sites.** Company-domain emails were MX-checked and
four were nulled because the domain cannot receive mail at all: `info@dulkius.lt`,
`info@mobeta.lt`, `info@magista.lt`, `info@rcc.lt` (parked, MX = `mx-parked.serveriai.lt`).
A dead email wastes the same outreach attempt as a wrong phone.

**A directory email that pointed at the wrong company.** BŪSTO APLINKA's listed domain
`manoaplinka.eu` now serves **UAB Unitechna**, a Kaunas petrol-station equipment firm. Website
and email nulled, phone kept.

**An identity fix.** visalietuva attached `valoresta.lt` to "VALORESA, UAB" (code 304284241,
Pulko g. 12A). The site itself says the cleaning business is **Business Man Holding, MB, code
305950059, Likiškėlių g. 104A, Alytus**, with the same phone. The site's version shipped.

**Geocoding was verified, not trusted.** Every row was geocoded from its own street address via
Photon with a Nominatim fallback and compared against the source pin. The override rule is
deliberately conservative: a geocode replaces the source pin **only** when it matches the same
house number in the same town and is within 25 km. That rule was written after a first pass moved
rows wrongly — `L. Giros g. 106` had been "corrected" 6.5 km to a house numbered 5A, and
`S. Nėries g. 105` 7.7 km to number 32.

**Four rows keep a source pin that no independent geocoder could confirm** (all are in the correct
municipality; the disagreement is intra-city): `rami-galva-vilnius`, `service-state-vilnius`,
`dllv-klaipedos-r` (the geocoder wanted Pagryniai, 48.8 km away and in the wrong district — the
source is right), `krovada-kauno-r`. Worth an eyeball on the map before outreach.

**Three pairs of rows legitimately share a coordinate** because two different businesses occupy
one building — Eišiškių pl. 47 Vilnius (Litperlas + Vikrova), Chemijos g. 27C Kaunas (Švaros
spindulys + Vallo pro), Eduardo Andrė g. 14 Vilnius (2 Draugai veža + Paslaugera). Each pair has
distinct names, phones and websites. `ad-rem-transport-vilnius` likewise shares
J. Dobkevičiaus g. 7 with wave 1's `UAB "Vilniaus tranzitas"` — different companies, different
registry codes, same logistics address.

**Chain branches are not duplicates.** The merge originally rejected 13 real regional branches
(Corpus A ×5, SOL Baltics ×3, Akvija ×3, Clean Solutions ×2) because they share a domain with the
head office already in the dataset. That was wrong, and matches how waves 1 and 2 treated Boxrent
(9 rows on one phone and one domain), Daiktams.lt (4) and Talga (3). **The dedupe rule is now:
a row's identity is its address.** A shared phone or domain at a *different* address is a branch;
only a match on address *plus* name/phone/domain is a duplicate. Independently confirmed
afterwards — Corpus A and Akvija use a different local landline in each town.

**Registry codes.** 80 rows carry one, all unique across wave 3 and against both existing files.
Where a chain publishes one code for many branches (Viada 178715423, Baltic Petroleum 111703588,
Saurida 266916280, Sivita, Corpus A) the code sits on **one** row and is null on the rest.

> **Pre-existing defect, still unfixed and repeated from wave 2's notes:** `lithuania.json`
> carries registry code **303083043 on three rows** (`daiktams-lt-vilnius-vilkpede`,
> `daiktams-lt-vilnius-fabijoniskes`, `daiktams-lt-kaunas`). That file will fail a unique index on
> `registryCode` on its own. Null two of the three before importing anything.

## 10. Search budget and what is left

**A ceiling was hit, but not the WebSearch one.** `info.lt` began returning **HTTP 429 to every
request** partway through the session and stayed there for the rest of it — roughly 20 retries
over 15 minutes with both plain and browser-like headers, plus later attempts an hour apart. All
info.lt evidence in this file therefore comes from the national dump cached earlier, which is
itself capped: rubrika 100219488 stopped at exactly 2,000 of 2,177 records, and the trailer
rubrika 100221793 yielded only 20 of its 91 rows before the block.

**Known remaining supply, in rough order of value:**

1. **Alauša** (Utena chain) states on its own site that almost all its stations rent trailers, but
   the station list loads from an `admin-ajax` endpoint that could not be reached. That chain
   covers the Utena / Anykščiai / Zarasai belt and is the obvious next harvest, alongside
   **Hovus** (60+ points, never reachable in any wave).
2. **info.lt rubrikas never scraped:** `100209636` Sandėliavimas/įranga, `100209603` Statybinės
   technikos ir įrankių nuoma (the tool-hire shops that rent trailers in small towns), and
   `100209516` Degalinės. Plus the 71 unreached rows of rubrika 100221793.
3. **paslaugos.lt** — 359 cleaners and 331 movers listed by town. Needs a way past the bot wall on
   detail pages, and an address source, since listings give only a city.
4. **45 removals firms** found in info.lt with a phone but **no address at all**, and one website
   between them. These are the one-van operators wave 2 described as existing only on Facebook.
   They are real supply and completely unreachable by crawling; they need a human or a Facebook
   search, not another scrape.
5. **175 info.lt companies** under the parent rubrika "Valymas, skalbimas" with **no
   sub-classification and no website** — the directory itself does not know whether they are
   premises cleaners or laundries. Deliberately **not** converted: with an exclusion rule that
   turns on exactly that distinction, shipping them would have been padding.

Items 4 and 5 together are ~220 businesses that are probably real but cannot be verified by any
method available here. That is the honest edge of what crawling can do for Lithuania.
